BIG YARDS / BUYING GUIDE

Best robot mowers for big yards.

Three large-lawn machines to compare, and the questions to ask before handing a robot the grass.

UPDATED OCTOBER 20268 MIN READ
EDITORIAL ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LISTED MODEL

A robot mower can make sense on a large lawn. But the advertised acreage is only one part of the story. Terrain, obstacles, charging time, and setup can turn a generous coverage claim into a tight schedule.

How this guide was made

This is a comparison of published manufacturer specifications, not a hands-on test or a ranking of real-world cut quality. Coverage figures are maker claims; actual results depend on your yard, weather, and mowing schedule. Confirm the exact model and installation requirements before buying.

The short answer

For a big, open property, start with a model whose stated capacity leaves breathing room beyond your measured mowing area. For uneven ground, check slope ratings and traction. For complicated zones and tree cover, learn how the machine navigates and what its setup requires.

Three models worth comparing

01

Husqvarna Automower 450XH EPOS

BEST FIT: LARGE LAWNS WITH A PROFESSIONAL SETUP PATH

Husqvarna lists a 2.5-acre working area for its 450XH EPOS kit. Its support specifications state 45% maximum slope performance inside the installation and describe virtual boundaries. Compare the total installed system, including the reference station and local support, rather than the mower alone.

02

Segway Navimow X390

BEST FIT: WIRE-FREE LARGE-LAWN COMPARISON

Segway's Navimow range lists the X390 for lawns up to 2.5 acres. The X3 family uses wire-free navigation, and Segway presents the series as a large-lawn option. Before choosing it, check placement needs for the antenna, site visibility, and how the obstacle system handles your particular yard.

03

Mammotion LUBA 2 AWD 10000HX

BEST FIT: LARGE, UNEVEN PROPERTIES

Mammotion lists the 10000HX variant for up to 2.5 acres and advertises all-wheel drive for demanding terrain. Its stated slope capability is up to 80%; that is a manufacturer claim, not our measured result. Check the high-cut version's cutting-height range and the support options available in your area.

Before you buy, map the yard

Measure mowable grass, not the property line

Acreage on a deed includes the house, beds, driveway, and woodland. Measure the lawn the mower will actually cover, then leave a capacity margin for rain delays and growth spurts.

Walk the worst slope

Published slope ratings may distinguish between inside the work area and at the boundary. Find the steepest section, especially around edges and transitions, and compare the relevant rating.

Plan for setup and service

Wire-free systems still require careful setup. Trees, buildings, and narrow passages can complicate positioning. Ask about local service, replacement blades, batteries, and what happens when the mower gets stuck.

Our take

If your lawn is close to two acres or more, compare the 2.5-acre class first and consider how much operating margin each machine leaves. The Husqvarna is worth a look when installer support matters; the Segway is a wire-free large-lawn contender; and the Mammotion merits comparison on rougher ground. A site plan will tell you more than a glossy acreage badge.
